# V9 full-prototype joint review

## What the physical trial established

The channel slides on by hand, remains wrapped around the rail while moved in X/Y, and can rock slightly. This supports retaining the current clearance for the full-assembly experiment. It does not quantify stiffness, four-joint alignment, uplift behavior or strength. More contact points do not eliminate clearance.

## Root and shoulder decisions

The 20 mm-long engagement, 14 mm T head, 7 mm stem, 3.2 mm channel side walls and 6 mm rear wall are retained. Nominal clearance is 0.3 mm per axis-aligned mating face and about 0.42 mm normal to the 45-degree shoulders. The section is constant along the vertical sliding direction; it does not wedge tighter as it seats.

The C shoulders oppose backward separation by bearing on the T shoulders. Their slope produces a spreading component in the channel walls, so shoulder/root cracking remains an inspection item. Side faces transfer across-width forces. The short 11 x 6 mm nominal seating patch on each ledge stops downward travel, but cannot provide tensile vertical restraint. Four such joints therefore cannot be modeled as four fully fixed or pinned connections: upward contact can open.

A 10.5 x 6 x 26 mm backing envelope is added to each rail root, mostly overlapping existing support material. It connects the previously short embedded stem to more of the rear rail. Its rear edge stops at the original rear-face plane; the moving channel starts 0.7 mm behind that plane. Only about 183 mm³ net material is added per support. This is a geometric load-path improvement, not a measured strength gain. No mating face or existing support member is cut.

The original 8 x 6 mm X members are retained in the brace, with a fused central crossing and broad overlap into the four channel backs. These distribute racking through axial forces and local bending, but slip and uplift may dominate apparent stiffness. The existing top geometry and calibrated tab/slot fit are preserved.

## Why this is a prototype release

The full model has no interference along the intended assembly paths. Connectivity and bed envelopes have been checked. That is enough to build the assembly-test prototype following the successful local fit test. It is not enough to assign a load rating.

No FEA, printed-material allowable stress, fatigue qualification, creep prediction or anti-lift validation was performed for this release. The earlier V7 idealized frame calculation assumed more constrained joints and cannot establish the V9 gravity-seated frame's stiffness or strength. In particular, neither self-weight nor a nominal 20 mm engagement proves that a connection cannot climb under cyclic racking.

The useful next observation is the assembled response: four-point seating, clearance take-up versus elastic movement, and any upward motion of a corner. If the frame climbs, a simple anti-lift provision would be justified by observed behavior; it has not been added preemptively.
